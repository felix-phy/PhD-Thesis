\chapter{Conclusion}
In this thesis, a first-time analysis of the cH process in the $H\rightarrow ZZ \rightarrow 4\mu$ channel is presented, using the 2018 dataset of the CMS detector, with the ultimate goal of placing constraints on the charm quark Yukawa interaction modifier \kappac. In \autoref{chap:Introduction}, the Standard Model of particle physics is discussed with a particular focus on the Higgs boson, as it is the Higgs boson which is responsible for the generation of particle masses in the SM. This is also true for the charm quark, which gains its mass through a Yukawa interaction with the Higgs boson. Since this interaction ist yet to be verified experimentally like it has been for other heavier particles in the SM, the cH process is identified as a process that is of experimental interest to this end. This process is discussed in a SM context and the method by which a signal strength measurement of the cH process can be translated to a contraint on \kappac. Subsequently, the interpretation of the cH process in a beyond-the-SM physics scenario, parametrised through SMEFT, is discussed. Specifically, the chronomagnetic dipole operator is identified as a SMEFT operator that may be uniquely contrained with the cH process, motivating the extension of a cH process analysis to additionally target such a SMEFT interpretation. \\
\\
In \autoref{chap:CMS}, the CMS detector, which can reconstruct pp collisions at the LHC to identify cH candidate events, is introduced. This includes a discussion of the various subdetector systems, as well as the reconstruction methods relevant to the analysis. Additionally, the jet flavour tagging algorithm \textsf{DeepJet} is introduced, along with the Monte Carlo simulation methods used to estimate the physics processes that occur at the LHC. The full analysis strategy, targetting specifically the \cHZZ channel in the SM, is detailed in \autoref{chap:cHAnalysis}. This includes the reconstruction of Higgs boson candidates and the selection of a jet candidate to reconstruct \cHZZ candidate events, with a particular focus on the selection of the latter. Subsequently, the Monte Carlo estimation of the \cHZZ signal as well as a number of background processes is discussed. A data-driven estimation of background processes not estimated via simulation is also presented. This is followed by a discussion of the statistical methods used to derive expected 95\% CL \CLs upper limit on the signal strength of the \cHZZ process, as well as the model of systematic uncertainty sources that is employed. Finally, upper limits on the \cHZZ signal strength and \kappac are set for a number of scenarios. In the nominal scenario, this corresponds to an expected upper limit of \sBR$= 1073$ ($\kappa_c = 189$). Additional scenarios using different discriminators are considered and for the nominal scenario, a statistical extrapolation to a full Run-2 and the anticipated High Luminosity-LHC datasets are performed. The corresponding expected upper limits are set at \sBR$ = 318$ ($\kappa_c = 58$) and \sBR$ = 158$ ($\kappa_c = 30$), respectively. Additionally, the impact of leading systematic uncertainty sources are discussed along with potential analysis improvements. Finally, in \autoref{chap:EFTAnalysis}, the potential of the presented analysis to constrain the Wilson coefficient of the chronomagnetic dipole operator in a SMEFT comtext is demonstrated and discussed. An expected constraint of $C_{cG} < 1.94$ is set at 95\% CL using the \CLs method. Similarly, a statistical extrapolation to full Run-2 and High Luminosity-LHC scenarios is performed, with the expected constraints being set at $C_{cG} < 1.58$ and $C_{cG} < 0.75$. Potential improvements to this interpretation of the analysis are also discussed. \\
\\
While the derived, expected analysis sensitivity to \kappac is lower than in other analyses of the cH(WW), cH($\gamma\gamma)$ processes as well as analyses targetting H$\rightarrow \mathrm{c\overline{c}}$ decays, it remains to be an important, complimentary contribution in the quest to determine the nature of the charm quark Yukawa coupling. Given the strong statistical limitations encoutered in all of the named analyses, a combination of many different analysis channels is expected to be an integral part in an ultimate measurement of this coupling, as we look towards future, larger datasets such as that of the HL-LHC to do so. As a result, contributions from less sensitive analysis channels remain valuable. Additionally, the SMEFT interpretation of the cH process presented here may provide exciting hints at deviations from the expected SM behaviour and represents a unique method with which to probe SMEFT operators.
