\chapter{An EFT interpretation of the cH(ZZ$\rightarrow$4$\mu$) process}
\label{chap:EFTAnalysis}
The methods developed in \autoref{chap:cHAnalysis} may also be used to investigate EFT interpretations of the cH signal. In this chapter, a demonstration of this idea is presented by targeting a modified cH process in which the CMD SMEFT operator, as discussed in \autoref{sec:CMDOperator}, is introduced. This follows the ideas presented in \cite{PhenoPaper}. Since the vast majority of analysis components remain unchanged from the SM interpretation of the \cHZZ analysis, only the relevant changes to this variant of the analysis are explicitly discussed. This includes a brief discussion in \autoref{sec:CMDSimulation} on the simulation of the signal associated with the CMD operator, here referred to as \cHZZCMD, and how this motivates a change in the jet selection strategy. Subsequently, a limit is set on the signal strength of this signal in \autoref{EFTResults}, which is interpreted as 95\% \CLs CL upper limit on the Wilson coefficient $C_{cG}$.

\section{The \cHZZCMD process}
\label{sec:CMDSimulation}
\subsection{Simulation of the \cHZZCMD process}
Like in the SM case, the signal associated with the $\hat{O}_{cG}$ operator is simulated using \textsf{MadGraph5\_aMC@NLO}. To specifically simulate SMEFT, the \textsf{SMEFTSim} model \cite{SMEFTSim1}\cite{SMEFTSim2} is used, which provides an implementation of dimension six SMEFT operators in the Warsaw basis. For this analysis, the contributions of all SMEFT operators except for the $\hat{O}_{cG}$ operator are turned off. While the \textsf{MadGraph5\_aMC@NLO} syntax used for the event generation is very similar to that presented in \autoref{sec:cHSimulation}, a key difference is that unlike in the SM, SMEFT models such as this one are curently limited to providing simulations at LO in QCD. A consequence of this is that in the simulation of the \cHZZCMD process, the ambiguity associated with a choice of flavour scheme in the simulation falls away. This is as a 3FS version of the \cHZZCMD process cannot be generated due to the process only being defined at NLO in QCD in the 3FS. Accordingly, the 4FS becomes the default choice. \\
\\
The simulation of the \cHZZCMD process consists of three components, the first of which is the purely SM component. This consists of the SM \cHZZ process, simulated with a cross section that is fixed to the SM expectation. Second is the so-called quadratic component, which consists exlusively of SMEFT contributions introduced by the $\hat{O}_{cG}$ operator. The last component is the so-called interference term, consisting of a mix of SM and SMEFT contributions. The effect of this behaviour behaviour on the \cHZZCMD cross section $\sigma_{total}$ can be parametrised as
\begin{align}
\label{eq:CMDScaling}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{total}} = A \sigma_{\mathrm{SM}} + B \sigma_{\mathrm{int.}} + C \sigma_{\mathrm{EFT}},
\end{align}
where A, B and C are coefficients describing the relative strength of each contribution. This expression can be normalised to the SM cH cross section and expressed in terms of $C_{cG}$ and the new physics scale $\Lambda$ as 
\begin{align}
\label{eq:CMDScaling}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{EFT}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{SM}} (1 +  \frac{C_{cG}}{\Lambda^2} \mu_{\mathrm{int.}} + \frac{C_{cG}}{\Lambda^2} \mu_{\mathrm{EFT}}),
\end{align}
introducing the signal strength modifiers $\mu_{\mathrm{int.}}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{EFT}}$ for the interference and quadratic terms respectively. By simulating the \cHZZCMD for a range of Wilson coefficient $C_{cG}$ values, the scaling behaviour of \autoref{eq:CMDScaling} with $C_{cG}$ can be obtained through a polynominal fit. Using the SM, LO in QCD cross section of $\sigma_{\mathrm{SM}} = 0.058$ pb and $\Lambda$ = 1 TeV, it is found that $\mu_{\mathrm{int.}}=2.2$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{EFT}}=55.8$ \cite{PhenoPaper}. \\
\\
A technical complication in setting upper limits on $C_{cG}$ arises from the fact that the interference and quadratic terms are, for some chosen discriminator, associated with different distribution shapes and their relationship is determined by the value of $C_{cG}$. This produces a non-trivial effect when scaling the signal with $C_{cG}$, such that the scaling produces not only cross section changes but also changes in the shape of the discriminator distribution. To address this, a simplification is made here. Given the relatively small size of the interference signal strength in comparison to the quadratic signal strength, the interference term is neglected and the total contribution sensitive to $C_{cG}$ is approximated using just the quadratic term. This allows for a simple scaling of the cross section of the signal dsitribution during the limit setting procedure, to which the SM component now becomes a background process. It should be noted that this only holds true for $C_{cG} > 1$ and as such, this approximation is only valid when probing this part of the parameter space in the limit setting procedure. 

\subsection{Modifications to the analysis strategy}
The event selection used in this analysis variant is kept almost identical to the selection presented in \autoref{sec:Selection}. The only change that is made is that a simplification with respect to the jet selection is made. Instead of applying the previously introduced jet selection alogrithm, the jet with the highest transverse momentum a candidate event is selected instead. This choice is motivated by the $\hat{O}_{cG}$ operator introducing a significant kinematic modification to the cH process in which the associated charm quarks can be produced at much higher transverse momenta than in the SM case. This behaviour can be seen in \autoref{fig:SMEFTpT} in a simulation of the \cHZZCMD process. The transverse momentum of the jet that is selected in this way for \cHZZCMD candidates can be seen in \autoref{fig:jet_ptEFT}, along with the full background estimation. Clearly, the \cHZZCMD produces a strong enhancement in the high transverse momentum regime of the selected jet, as expected. This quality makes the transverse momentum of the selected jet an excellent candidate as the final discriminator in the statistical evaluation. The background processes considered here are the same as in \autoref{chap:cHAnalysis}, with the exception being that the SM component of the \cHZZ process is now also considered a background process. \\

The same statistical evaluation strategy as presented in is used \autoref{chap:cHAnalysis}. Again, a range of systematic uncertainty sources are considered in the uncertainty model. This includes all the previously introduced uncertainty sources, with the exception of those associated with jet flavour tagging, since not jet flavour tagging is performed. Since only the jet selection method has changed, the relative magnitude of the uncertainties remains unchanged with respect to the numbers quoted in \autoref{chap:cHAnalysis}. For the \cHZZCMD signal simulation, the same uncertainty sources as for the \cHZZ process are considered aisde from the flavour scheme uncertainty, which is not defined for the  \cHZZCMD signal simulation. The magnitude of the theoretical uncertainties on the \cHZZCMD signal are, like for the \cHZZ process, significant. This encompasses especially the uncertainty associated with the PDF set, which produces a yield change of approximately 20\%, as well as the renormalisation and factorisation scales that are associated with yield changes of approximately 18\% and 10\%, respectively.
\section{Results}
\label{EFTResults}
Using the tranverse momentum of the selected jet as a final discriminator, which corresponds to the distribution shown in \autoref{fig:jet_ptEFT}, expected upper limits on $C_{cG}$ are set. To ensure compatibility with the perturbativity requirements discussed in \autoref{sec:EFTValidty}, a $M_{cut}$ = 1 TeV criterium is applied to candidate events. Here $M_{cut}$ is estimated via the invariant mass of the Higgs boson candidate and jet system. At a luminosity equivalent to the 2018 dataset of the CMS detector, an expected upper limit of $C_{cG} < 1.94$ is set. \\
\\
Like for the SM oriented analysis, a decomposition of the effects of the individual uncertainty sources on this result can be performed. Again, the 30 leading uncertainty sources are considered for a signal strength corresponding to the set upper limit on $C_{cG}$, which are listed in \autoref{fig:ImpactsSM}. Leading theoretical uncertainties include the uncertainty associated with the PDF used in simulation, the renormalisation and factorisation scale of the \cHZZCMD signal, as well the uncertainties associated with $\mathcal{B}(H \rightarrow ZZ \rightarrow 4\ell)$ and the qq$\rightarrow$ZZ k-factor. Leading experimental uncertainty include the normalisation of the reducible background estimation, the uncertainty on the luminosity as well as the calibration of the muons. A common factor to all these uncertaintiy sources is that they are associated with larger relative yield changes in the background and signal processes, which can potentially mask the presence of the signal. Also noticeable is that due to the sparse population of bins in the discriminator bins at very high transverse momenta, the statistical modelling uncertainties associated with these bins is also significant. \\

A statistical extrapolation to full Run-2 and and HL-LHC scenario is again performed. In a Run-2 scenario, an expected upper limit of $C_{cG} < 1.58$ is derived. For a HL-LHC scenario, this improves to $C_{cG} < 0.75$. It should be noted that in this regime, the contribution from the linear term associated with $\hat{O}_{cG}$ becomes increasingly significant and must be accounted for. \\
\\
Given that the presented analysis serves as a basic demonstration, it is clear that a number of potential improvements can be made in future analysis iterations. Like in the SM case, methods with which to increase the overall signal selection efficiency would be interesting to explore. While the low transverse momentum region of the jet is not relvant in the way it was previously, the loosening of the muon selection criteria used in the Higgs boson candidate reconstruction is again of interest. Additionally, other selection optimisations could be explored, such as multivariate methods. Since theoretical uncertainties associated with the modelling of the background and signal processes again constrains the analysis sensitivity significantly, improvements in this domain are also of significant interest here.
