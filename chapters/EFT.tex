\chapter{An EFT interpretation of the cH(ZZ$\rightarrow$4$\mu$) process}
\label{chap:EFTAnalysis}
In this chapter, a variation of the analysis developed in \autoref{chap:cHAnalysis} is presented. In this version of the analysis, the process associated with the CMD EFT operator discussed in \autoref{sec:CMDOperator} is targeted as the signal of interest instead of the SM \cHZZ process. The cross section of the latter is set to the SM expectation and it is treated as a background process to the CMD operator signal. This analysis is intended to serve as a simple. non-exhaustive demonstration of the ideas presented in \cite{PhenoPaper}, here applied in the context of CMS detector data. Since the vast majority of analysis components remain unchanged from the SM \cHZZ analysis, only the relevant changes to this variant of the analysis are explicitly discussed. This includes a brief discussion in \autoref{sec:CMDSimulation} on the simulation of the signal associated with the CMD operator, here referred to as \cHZZCMD, and how this motivates a change in the jet selection strategy. Subsequently, a limit is set on the signal strength of this signal in \autoref{EFTResults}, which is interpreted as 95\% \CLs CL upper limit on the Wilson coefficient $C_{cG}$.

\section{The \cHZZCMD process}
\label{sec:CMDSimulation}
\subsection{Simulation of the \cHZZCMD process}
Like in the SM case, the signal associated with the $\hat{O}_{cG}$ operator is simulated using \textsf{MadGraph5\_aMC@NLO}. To specifically simulate SMEFT, the \textsf{SMEFTSim} model \cite{SMEFTSim1}\cite{SMEFTSim2} is used, which provides an implementation of dimension six SMEFT operators in the Warsaw basis. For this analysis, the contributions of all SMEFT operators except for the $\hat{O}_{cG}$ operator are turned off. While the \textsf{MadGraph5\_aMC@NLO} syntax used for the event generation is very similar to that presented in \autoref{sec:cHSimulation}, a key difference is that unlike in the SM, SMEFT models such as this one are curently limited to providing simulations at LO in QCD. A consequence of this is that in the simulation of the \cHZZCMD process, the ambiguity associated with a choice of flavour scheme in the simulation falls away. This is as a 3FS version of the \cHZZCMD process cannot be generated due to the process only being defined at NLO in QCD in the 3FS. Accordingly, the 4FS becomes the default choice. \\
\\
The simulation of the \cHZZCMD process consists of three components, the first of which is the purely SM component. This consists of the SM \cHZZ process, simulated with a cross section that is fixed to the SM expectation. Second is the so-called quadratic component, which consists exlusively of SMEFT contributions introduced by the $\hat{O}_{cG}$ operator. The last component is the so-called interference term, consisting of a mix of SM and SMEFT contributions. The effect of this behaviour behaviour on the \cHZZCMD cross section $\sigma_{total}$ can be parametrised as

\begin{align}
\label{eq:CMDScaling}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{total}} = A \sigma_{\mathrm{SM}} + B \sigma_{\mathrm{int.}} + C \sigma_{\mathrm{EFT}},
\end{align}
where A, B and C are coefficients describing the relative strength of each contribution. This expression can be normalised to the SM cH cross section and expressed in terms of $C_{cG}$ and the new physics scale $\Lambda$ as 
\begin{align}
\label{eq:CMDScaling}
    \sigma_{\mathrm{EFT}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{SM}} (1 +  \frac{C_{cG}}{\Lambda^2} \mu_{\mathrm{int.}} + \frac{C_{cG}}{\Lambda^2} \mu_{\mathrm{EFT}}),
\end{align}
introducing the signal strength modifiers $\mu_{\mathrm{int.}}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{EFT}}$ for the interference and quadratic terms respectively. By simulating the \cHZZCMD for a range of Wilson coefficient $C_{cG}$ values, the scaling behaviour of \autoref{eq:CMDScaling} with $C_{cG}$ can be obtained through a polynominal fit. Using the SM, LO in QCD cross section of $\sigma_{\mathrm{SM}} = 0.058$ pb and $\Lambda$ = 1 TeV, it is found that $\mu_{\mathrm{int.}}=2.2$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{EFT}}=55.8$ \cite{PhenoPaper}. \\
\\
A significant complication in setting upper limits on $C_{cG}$ arises from the fact that the interference and quadratic terms are, for some chosen discriminator, associated with different distribution shapes and their relationship is determined by the value of $C_{cG}$. This produces a non-trivial effect when scaling the signal with $C_{cG}$, such that the scaling produces not only cross section changes but also changes in the shape of the discriminator distribution. To combat this, a simplification is made for this analysis. Given the relatively small size of the interference signal strength in comparison to the quadratic signal strength, the interference term is neglected and the total contribution sensitive to $C_{cG}$ is approximated using just the quadratic term. This allows for a simple scaling of the cross section of the signal dsitribution during the limit setting procedure, to which the SM component now becomes a background process. It should be noted that this only holds true for $C_{cG} > 1$ and as such, this approximation is only valid when probing this part of the parameter space in the limit setting procedure. 

\subsection{Analysis strategy}
The event selection used in this analysis variant is kept almost identical to the selection presented in \autoref{sec:Selection}. The only change that is made is that a simplification with respect to the jet selection is made. Instead of applying the previously introduced jet selection alogrithm, the jet with the highest transverse momentum a candidate event is selected instead. This choice is motivated by the $\hat{O}_{cG}$ operator operator introducing a significant kinematic modification to the cH process in which the associated charm quarks can be produced at much higher transverse momenta than in the SM case.
